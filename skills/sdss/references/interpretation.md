# Reading the measurements

Keep the source values, including nulls and sentinel values, in machine-readable evidence. SDSS commonly uses -999 for unavailable values; it is not a measurement. `sdss_id=-999` cannot identify a target. Zero photometric specObjID means no linked legacy spectrum. Distinguish missing associations, unsupported parameter joins and genuinely queried empty results.

Spectroscopic `class` and `subclass` are pipeline fits, not imaging labels. The helper retains raw `z`, `z_err` and `zwarning`. Negative or missing errors and flags are unavailable; nonzero zwarning requires the applicable bit definitions and investigation. An unflagged pipeline fit can still be wrong. [DR20 BHM redshift guidance](https://www.sdss.org/dr20/bhm/data/redshifts/) recommends the visual-inspection and reliability VAC over raw spAll results; that VAC is not included here. Consult [BHM caveats](https://www.sdss.org/dr20/bhm/caveats/) and [legacy spectroscopic caveats](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/spectro/caveats/) for artifacts, template failures and noise limitations.

Repeated spectral records may be different observations, daily/epoch coadds, instruments or pipelines. Compare exact IDs and provenance; do not collapse them into a single “latest truth” or count them as distinct galaxies. `archive_release` is extracted from the SAS URL, while `catalog_release` describes the querying catalog. Legacy DR17 products appear in DR20 ALLSPEC. The record's `sdss_phase`, `run2d`, `coadd`, MJD and URL retain additional provenance.

DR17 r-band magnitudes are SDSS asinh magnitudes. PSF and cModel estimate different quantities; label the method, band, measurement error and whether extinction was subtracted. The helper returns observed magnitudes plus extinction; it does not deredden them. `clean=1` is a pipeline imaging quality selection, not spectroscopic certainty. `type=6` is point-like and `type=3` is extended morphology; neither certifies physical STAR/GALAXY. Read the [DR17 magnitudes documentation](https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/algorithms/magnitudes/) before comparing or converting faint measurements.

For legacy and BOSS scalar optical spec FITS, the COADD table generally contains `loglam`, `flux`, `ivar`, `and_mask` and `or_mask`. Use the actual file header and [official optical data model](https://data.sdss.org/datamodel/files/BOSS_SPECTRO_REDUX/RUN2D/spectra/lite/PLATE4/spec.html), with [BHM spectra documentation](https://www.sdss.org/dr20/bhm/data/spectra/) for current products. Wavelength is observed vacuum Angstrom, usually `10**loglam`; flux density is in 10^-17 erg cm^-2 s^-1 Angstrom^-1. Keep negative flux. Positive finite ivar gives marginal sigma `1/sqrt(ivar)`; zero ivar means no usable uncertainty. Retain both masks; any mask exclusion is an explicit analysis policy. Neighbor covariance and systematics limit line-significance claims. Do not apply these scalar optical rules to APOGEE, MaNGA or LVM without their own data models.

Use a maintained FITS reader such as Astropy for decoding, installed in an isolated analysis environment when needed. The standard-library helper downloads original files and deliberately does not parse FITS arrays. In BOSS FPS files, primary-header RA/DEC can describe the field pointing; retain the catalog target coordinates and inspect target-specific SPALL or PLUG_RA/PLUG_DEC fields before assigning a spectrum's sky position. Distances and lookback times require a stated cosmological model and a scientifically appropriate redshift; stellar radial velocity is not Hubble distance.
